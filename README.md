39 tools for a support team, grouped by what they do to a ticket: where it lands, whether it
is answered without a person, what the person answering can see, how the answer is checked,
and how many people are rostered to write it.

One view runs through the list: deflection is not resolution. A ticket that never reached an
agent may be a customer who was helped or a customer who gave up, and a number that counts
both is not a measure of anything. Entries are described by what they do when they cannot
answer.

Maintained by [TiorAI](https://tiorai.com/), which catalogues AI tools for a living.

## Help desks

Where the ticket lands, and what the agent sees when it does.

- **[Dixa](https://www.dixa.com/)** — Treats a customer as one thread across channels rather than one ticket per message. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Freshdesk](https://www.freshworks.com/freshdesk/)** — A free tier that covers a real small team, which almost nothing else in this category offers. `Freemium` `Web` `iOS` `Android` `API`
- **[Front](https://front.com/)** — A shared inbox that still reads like email, which suits teams whose customers know them. `Paid` `Web` `Windows` `macOS` `iOS` `Android`
- **[Gladly](https://www.gladly.ai/)** — Organised around the person rather than the case, which changes how repeat contact reads. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Help Scout](https://www.helpscout.com/)** — Deliberately small in features, which is why a team of five can run it without training. `Paid` `Web` `iOS` `Android` `API`
- **[Hiver](https://www.hiverhq.com/)** — Runs the queue inside Gmail, so nobody has to be moved to a new tool. `Paid` `Web` `Browser Extension`
- **[Kustomer](https://www.kustomer.com/)** — Built around a customer timeline that pulls in order and account data as context. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Zendesk](https://www.zendesk.com/)** — The default a growing team is asked to standardise on, with the largest app directory. `Paid` `Web` `iOS` `Android` `API`

## Resolution agents

Software that closes the ticket instead of routing it.

- **[Ada](https://www.ada.cx/)** — Resolution-priced automation across chat, email, and voice from one knowledge source. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Decagon](https://www.decagon.ai/)** — Agents that take actions in your systems rather than only answering questions. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/)** — Trains on the help desk's own past tickets, which is the corpus nobody else uses. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Forethought](https://www.forethought.ai/)** — Predicts intent before routing, so the deflection happens ahead of the queue. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Intercom Fin](https://fin.ai/)** — Charged only per conversation it actually resolved, which is an unusually honest model. `Paid` `Web` `API`

## Voice and phone support

The channel customers still use when the answer matters.

- **[Aircall](https://www.aircall.io/)** — A phone system that plugs into the help desk rather than sitting beside it. `Paid` `Web` `iOS` `Android` `API`
- **[Cresta](https://www.cresta.com/)** — Coaches the agent during the call, not in a review a week later. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Five9](https://www.five9.com/)** — A contact centre platform with routing built for teams measured on queue time. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Genesys](https://www.genesys.com/)** — The enterprise end, and the one a large organisation's procurement already knows. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Talkdesk](https://www.talkdesk.com/)** — Industry-specific packages, which matters where regulation shapes what an agent may say. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Verint](https://www.verint.com/)** — Analyses recorded calls at archive scale, which is what compliance reviews actually need. `Paid` `Web` `API`

## Knowledge bases

The article the answer comes from, and whether anyone can find it.

- **[Document360](https://document360.com/)** — Versioned documentation with a workflow, aimed at products that ship releases. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Guru](https://www.getguru.com/)** — Surfaces the card inside the tool an agent is already in, and expires it when it goes stale. `Freemium` `Web` `Browser Extension`
- **[Helpjuice](https://www.helpjuice.com/)** — Analytics on what was searched and not found, which is the list of articles to write next. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Stonly](https://www.stonly.com/)** — Step-by-step guides rather than articles, which resolve where a wall of text does not. `Paid` `Web`

## Quality and coaching

Reading what agents actually sent, at more than a two-percent sample.

- **[Level AI](https://www.level.ai/)** — Checks compliance language on every call, which is a legal requirement in some sectors. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[MaestroQA](https://www.maestroqa.com/)** — Builds the scorecard around your own rubric rather than a supplied one. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Observe.AI](https://www.observe.ai/)** — Ties quality scores to outcomes, so coaching targets what changed a result. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Playvox](https://www.playvox.com/)** — Quality, coaching, and scheduling bought together rather than as three contracts. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Zendesk QA](https://www.zendesk.com/service/quality-assurance/)** — Scores every conversation rather than the handful a manager had time to open. `Paid` `Web` `API`

## How tools are selected

A tool qualifies when it changes what happens to a **ticket**: where it lands, whether it gets
answered without a person, what the person answering can see, how the answer is checked
afterwards, or how many people are rostered to write it. It has to be sold to support teams
rather than adapted by them.

### The rule that does most of the work here

**Deflection is not resolution, and the list refuses to treat them as the same thing.** A large
part of this market reports success as tickets that did not reach an agent. That number counts
the customer who gave up alongside the customer who was helped, and it is the metric that
produces the support experiences everybody complains about.

So the resolution-agent category is written around what a product does when it cannot answer:
whether it hands over cleanly, whether it says it does not know, whether the escalation carries
the conversation with it. A product that only reports a deflection rate is described as what it
is, and several that could not answer the question at all were left out.

The second filter is more ordinary. Support software is bought once and lived in for years, so a
product in closed beta or without published pricing is not something a team can act on, and
neither is here.

### What the categories are telling you

The seven groups are stages of a ticket rather than product categories, and two of them exist
because the market hides them:

- **Quality and coaching** is where support teams actually improve, and it is the line item cut
  first. Reading two percent of conversations by hand is the alternative most teams have.
- **Support for small teams** is separate because everything above it is priced per agent per
  month against a team that has agents. A company with one person answering needs a different
  shortlist, not a cheaper tier of the same one.

### What is excluded

Products in closed beta, on a waitlist, or with no published pricing at all. Generic chat
widgets with no knowledge source behind them. Sentiment scoring sold as a standalone product,
which is a feature rather than a tool. Outsourced support providers, which are a service.
Anything that only reports a deflection rate and cannot say what happens when it fails.

Sales software is out of scope even where the same vendor sells both; it belongs in
`awesome-ai-sales-tools`. Store-specific support apps live in `awesome-ai-tools-for-ecommerce`
and `awesome-ai-tools-for-shopify`, described for a merchant rather than for a support lead.

Entries are ordered alphabetically inside each category. Position carries no meaning, and
there is no paid placement, sponsorship, or affiliate link anywhere in this repository.

## Disclaimer

Everything listed is built and operated by a third party, not by TiorAI. Pricing, plan
limits, and terms change without notice, and a tool that fits today may not next quarter.

**Per-agent pricing and per-resolution pricing are not comparable, and vendors know it.** A tool
charged per seat costs the same whether it resolves anything; a tool charged per resolution
costs more the better it works. Model both against your own volume before you compare a
headline price with a headline price.

**Everything here reads your customers' messages.** That is what the software is for, and it
means every entry is a data-processing decision: where the conversations are stored, how long
for, whether they are used for training, and who the sub-processors are. None of that is in this
list because none of it is stable enough to publish. Ask the vendor, in writing, before the
trial.

Listing here is editorial selection, not endorsement.
